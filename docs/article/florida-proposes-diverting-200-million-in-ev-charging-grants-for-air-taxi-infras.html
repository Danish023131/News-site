Florida Proposes Diverting $200 Million in EV Charging Grants for Air Taxi Infrastructure

TechCrunch · Jul 31, 2026

State transportation authorities in Florida have initiated a controversial proposal to repurpose approximately $200 million in federal funding, which was originally allocated for electric vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure, toward the development of an advanced air taxi network. According to recent reports, the state aims to utilize these substantial resources to construct specialized landing pads, known as vertiports, designed to connect high-profile locations such as luxury residential complexes, major aviation hubs, and popular golf courses.

The federal funds in question were initially distributed under initiatives designed to expand green energy and support the widespread adoption of battery-powered automobiles across American highways. However, Florida officials argue that investing in vertical takeoff and landing (VTOL) technology could significantly alleviate regional traffic congestion and modernize the state's transportation ecosystem. Proponents of the plan envision a future where autonomous or piloted electric aircraft seamlessly shuttle affluent commuters and travelers between exclusive urban centers and key transit points.

This pivot has sparked significant debate among urban planners, environmental advocates, and federal regulators. Critics point out that shifting capital away from traditional EV chargers could slow down the necessary transition toward sustainable ground transportation, potentially leaving standard motorists with fewer charging stations than promised. On the other hand, aviation enthusiasts and tech sector investors view the move as a visionary step that positions Florida at the forefront of the burgeoning urban air mobility market.

As the discussion continues, the final approval of this fund reallocation remains under review by federal agencies. The outcome of this decision could set a precedent for how states manage federal green infrastructure grants, balancing the immediate needs of electric vehicle drivers against the futuristic ambitions of commercial air taxi services.
