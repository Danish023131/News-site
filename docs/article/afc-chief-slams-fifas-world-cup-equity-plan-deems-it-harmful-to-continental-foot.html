AFC Chief Slams FIFA’s World Cup Equity Plan, Deems It Harmful to Continental Football

Al Jazeera · Jul 30, 2026

The leadership of Asian football has strongly expressed its dissatisfaction with global governing body FIFA, intensifying a debate over the future commercial structure of the sport's most prestigious tournament. The head of the Asian Football Confederation (AFC) has publicly rejected recent proposals regarding the sale of tournament stakes, labeling the initiative as completely unsuitable for the current ecosystem of international football.

According to the AFC leadership, the strategy under consideration by FIFA poses a direct threat to the established continental framework that has long governed the sport. Critics argue that shifting financial models in this manner could weaken the influence and operational stability of regional confederations, which play a vital role in developing football from the grassroots level up to professional tiers.

The core of the disagreement lies in how commercial rights and tournament revenues are managed and distributed. While FIFA aims to expand its revenue streams and global financial footprint through new investment partnerships, regional bodies fear being sidelined in decision-making processes. The debate highlights a growing tension between global administrative ambitions and the regional governing bodies that represent local associations worldwide.

As discussions continue, sports analysts note that this rift could lead to broader governance challenges within international football. The opposition from Asia—one of the game's fastest-growing and most lucrative markets—adds significant pressure on FIFA to reconsider its commercial strategies and engage in deeper consultations with its member confederations before moving forward with any sweeping structural changes.
