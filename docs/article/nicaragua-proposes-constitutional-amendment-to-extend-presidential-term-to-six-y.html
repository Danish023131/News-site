Nicaragua Proposes Constitutional Amendment to Extend Presidential Term to Six Years

BBC World · Jul 30, 2026

In a significant political development, Nicaragua has put forward a fresh constitutional reform proposal aimed at lengthening the official presidential term from five to six years. This legislative move marks yet another structural adjustment in the Central American nation's governance framework under the administration of President Daniel Ortega.

Political analysts note that alterations to the country's foundational laws have occurred periodically over recent decades, often resulting in expanded executive authority and extended tenure possibilities for the current leadership. Critics and international observers have frequently expressed concern over these legal adjustments, arguing they weaken democratic checks and balances by concentrating power within the executive branch.

Supporters of the amendment, however, defend the initiative as a necessary measure to ensure governmental continuity and long-term strategic planning for national development projects. They maintain that the changes align with the country's sovereign right to shape its political institutions according to domestic priorities.

The proposed changes are expected to move through the legislative process in the coming weeks. Given the current political composition of the national assembly, the reform is anticipated to face minimal institutional resistance before potentially being formally integrated into the nation's legal framework. Meanwhile, international bodies and regional watchdogs continue to monitor the situation closely, assessing the broader implications for political stability and democratic norms in Nicaragua.
