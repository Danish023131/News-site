Google Fixes Record Chrome Bugs in June Using AI-Powered Tools

TechCrunch · Jul 31, 2026

Artificial intelligence continues to revolutionize the software development and cybersecurity landscapes. Recently, tech giant Google revealed a significant milestone in its ongoing security efforts, reporting that it successfully identified and resolved more bugs in its Chrome browser during the month of June than the cumulative total fixed over the preceding two years. This dramatic surge in vulnerability patching has been directly attributed to the integration of advanced Large Language Models (LLMs) and specialized AI tools.

For quite some time, industry experts and cybersecurity analysts have anticipated a paradigm shift in how digital products are maintained. Predictions made over the last couple of years suggested that automated intelligence would soon enable tech companies to scan, detect, and rectify software flaws at an exponential rate. Google's recent operational success validates these forecasts, demonstrating that artificial intelligence can drastically accelerate security maintenance.

Traditionally, software debugging has been a labor-intensive process requiring extensive manual code reviews by human engineers. While human oversight remains critical, AI-driven systems can now analyze massive codebases within seconds, identifying potential security loopholes and anomalies that might otherwise go unnoticed. Following similar initiatives by industry competitors like Microsoft, Google's aggressive deployment of LLMs highlights a broader trend across the technology sector. Companies are increasingly relying on machine learning to fortify their infrastructure against sophisticated cyber threats.

As these AI tools become more refined, users can anticipate safer digital environments and more frequent updates for widely used applications like Google Chrome. However, experts note that while AI greatly assists in finding and fixing internal vulnerabilities, threat actors are also utilizing similar technologies. Consequently, the cybersecurity race between automated defense mechanisms and malicious exploits is expected to intensify in the coming years.
