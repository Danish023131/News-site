Middle East Tension: Saudi Arabia and US Jointly Target Iran-Backed Militias in Iraq

BBC World · Jul 29, 2026

In a significant regional development, Saudi Arabian forces have reportedly joined the United States in executing targeted airstrikes against Iran-backed militant factions operating within Iraq. This military action comes in the wake of escalating security threats and persistent assaults directed at coalition military installations as well as vital Saudi energy infrastructure.

According to reports released by the US Central Command, the decision to launch these coordinated counter-strikes was prompted by a series of aggressive operations orchestrated by regional proxy groups. These hostile entities have increasingly targeted international defense assets and critical economic installations in the Gulf region, raising serious concerns regarding regional stability and the safety of global energy supplies.

Defense analysts note that Saudi Arabia's active participation marks a notable shift in the security dynamics of the area, reflecting a shared resolve among allied nations to deter further aggression by non-state actors linked to Tehran. The coordinated raids aim to dismantle the operational capabilities of these armed groups and curtail their capacity to execute future offensives against strategic targets.

International diplomats are closely monitoring the situation, expressing apprehension over the potential for further escalation in an already volatile Middle Eastern landscape. While coalition officials maintain that the military response is strictly defensive and necessary to protect personnel and assets, regional authorities continue to urge restraint to prevent a broader conflict.
