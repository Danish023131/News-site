Amtrak Coach vs Business Class: Why One Passenger Says the Upgrade Isn't Worth the Extra Cost

Business Insider · Jul 31, 2026

Planning a scenic rail journey through New York can be an exciting alternative to flying, but choosing the right class of service makes all the difference. Recently, one traveler took a round-trip Amtrak journey on the Maple Leaf line, experiencing coach service from New York City to Rochester and upgrading to business class for the return trip. The goal was simple: determine whether a $180 business-class ticket genuinely offers better value than a standard $69 coach seat for a multi-hour ride.

The initial leg in coach proved to be a peaceful and budget-friendly experience. Boarding at Moynihan Train Hall came with a minor delay, but once settled into the front car, the passenger enjoyed a quiet atmosphere and the luxury of claiming a two-seater row entirely to themselves. Despite some limitations with window views on the right side and a long walk to the rear café car, the ample legroom, reliable power outlets, and overall calm environment created a comfortable environment reminiscent of airplane economy minus the cramped quarters.

In contrast, the return journey in business class brought mixed results despite the higher price tag. Priced at $180, the ticket included wider reclining seats, extra legroom, a footrest, a complimentary non-alcoholic drink, and ticket refundability. However, because the business-class car is typically located at the very back of the train—right next to the caboose and café car—the ride was noticeably bumpier and louder. Furthermore, an extended stop in Albany due to a connecting train caused stuffy conditions on board, highlighting the lack of spacious seating rows where one could comfortably nap.

While the business class did offer perks like a free ginger ale and stunning sunset views of the Hudson River, the passenger concluded that the minor upgrades did not justify the steep price increase. With coach cars frequently offering vacant adjacent seats for at least part of the journey, future trips upstate will likely see this traveler saving money and sticking exclusively to coach.
