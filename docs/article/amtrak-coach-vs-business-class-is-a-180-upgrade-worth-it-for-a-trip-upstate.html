Amtrak Coach vs. Business Class: Is a $180 Upgrade Worth It for a Trip Upstate?

Business Insider · Jul 31, 2026

When planning a getaway from the chaotic energy of New York City to the peaceful landscapes of upstate Rochester, travelers often weigh the transportation options. Avoiding airport hassles makes train travel an attractive choice, but choosing the right class of service can significantly impact the experience. Recently, one traveler tested both options on a round-trip journey, taking a coach seat heading north and upgrading to business class for the return trip to determine if the extra expense is truly justified.

The journey in coach cost $69 for a roughly seven-hour ride on the Maple Leaf train. Although departure from Penn Station involved some minor scheduling delays, the coach car offered a surprisingly pleasant environment. Filled to about 60% capacity, the carriage allowed solo passengers to claim entire rows, providing ample legroom and a quiet space to work or relax. While the right-side window seat limited direct views of the Hudson River, the overall experience mirrored a spacious airline economy setup, complete with power outlets and convenient tray tables.

On the way back, a $180 business-class ticket promised larger reclining seats, a footrest, extra legroom, and a complimentary non-alcoholic beverage. However, the placement of the business-class car at the very rear of the train—right next to the café car—introduced unexpected drawbacks. Being in the caboose resulted in a noticeably bumpier ride, making screen time difficult and causing mild motion sickness. Furthermore, an extended stop in Albany due to a connecting train left passengers in a warm, stuffy car for nearly two hours, highlighting the flexibility that coach seating often provides for stretching out.

Ultimately, while business class offered perks like a free drink, a single-seat configuration, and stunning sunset views of the Hudson River during the final stretch, the minor upgrades failed to justify the steep price difference for a regional trip. Future travelers making the upstate trek might find better value in sticking with coach, saving the splurge for much longer cross-country routes that include full meal services.
