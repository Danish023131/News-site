LinkedIn Launches New Reporting Tool to Combat Low-Quality AI Content

TechCrunch · Jul 30, 2026

Professional networking platform LinkedIn is taking decisive steps to address the growing volume of low-value, automated content circulating on its feeds. In an effort to maintain professional discourse and user engagement, the platform has rolled out a dedicated reporting mechanism that allows users to flag generic or repetitive AI-generated material.

Under the updated reporting system, members can now select a specific option indicating that a post appears to be low-quality automated content, informally referred to as 'AI slop.' This move is designed to empower the user community to actively curate their feeds and help algorithms better identify and suppress unoriginal or spammy material that adds little value to professional conversations.

In tandem with this user-facing control, LinkedIn is also restructuring its internal generative AI offerings. The platform is phasing out its automated writing assistant, which previously allowed users to generate posts from scratch using artificial intelligence. Instead, the company is introducing a refined proofreading and editing tool. This shift emphasizes assistance over total automation, encouraging users to write their own content while leveraging AI strictly for grammar checks, tone adjustments, and clarity enhancements.

Industry analysts view these updates as part of a broader trend among major social and professional networks striving to balance the productivity benefits of generative AI with the risk of platform saturation. As AI tools become increasingly accessible, maintaining authenticity and meaningful human connection remains a primary challenge for digital platforms. By shifting away from automated content generation toward editorial enhancement, LinkedIn aims to foster a more genuine and trustworthy environment for its hundreds of millions of global professionals.
