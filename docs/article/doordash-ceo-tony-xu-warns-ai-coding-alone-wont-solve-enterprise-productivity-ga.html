DoorDash CEO Tony Xu Warns AI Coding Alone Won't Solve Enterprise Productivity Gaps

Business Insider · Jul 29, 2026

Artificial intelligence has transformed how developers write software, but according to DoorDash Chief Executive Officer Tony Xu, writing code is only a fraction of an engineer's daily responsibilities. Speaking recently on a podcast episode, Xu emphasized that while AI models are successfully generating a significant portion of the company's codebase, relying solely on coding assistants falls short of achieving maximum organizational efficiency.

At DoorDash, roughly two-thirds of the code is currently produced by AI models, delivering measurable productivity boosts. However, Xu pointed out that software engineers spend a large portion of their work hours outside of actual coding. Activities like participating in product reviews, holding design meetings, and aligning with various cross-functional business units take up substantial time. Without streamlining these collaborative and managerial workflows using AI as well, companies will struggle to realize their desired productivity gains.

Xu's perspective reflects a growing chorus of caution among major tech leaders regarding the current limitations of generative artificial intelligence. While tools capable of 'vibe coding' have captured widespread attention, industry executives are increasingly noting that these technologies do not automatically overhaul corporate structures or eliminate broader operational bottlenecks. For instance, Wix CEO Avishai Abrahami recently cautioned that the tech sector might be overestimating AI's short-term capabilities to replace human labor entirely, while former Tesla AI head Andrej Karpathy has pointed out that AI-generated code can often be bloated and brittle.

Beyond software development, Xu highlighted that DoorDash is also exploring physical AI applications, such as autonomous delivery vehicles and automated food-preparation hardware, to enhance other segments of its ecosystem. Despite the enthusiasm surrounding automated software generation, Xu maintains a pragmatic view, noting that true digital transformation requires businesses to become AI-native across their entire operational framework, rather than just within the engineering department.
