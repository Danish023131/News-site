Rising Temperatures in Europe Double Wildfire Risks Since 1980s, Study Reveals

Al Jazeera · Jul 30, 2026

A comprehensive new report has revealed that escalating global temperatures are driving a dramatic surge in wildfire activity across southern Europe. According to the latest scientific findings, the probability of destructive forest fires in the region has doubled over the past four decades, largely fueled by shifting climate patterns that began around 1981. Researchers point out that prolonged dry spells, record-breaking summer heatwaves, and increasingly arid landscapes have created an environment where blazes spread faster and are significantly harder to control.

The study underscores an urgent need for European nations to reassess and strengthen their current disaster management and prevention strategies. As seasonal temperatures continue to break historical records, traditional firefighting methods are proving insufficient against the scale and frequency of modern infernos. Environmental experts emphasize that without aggressive global action to curb greenhouse gas emissions and targeted regional adaptation measures, Mediterranean and southern European ecosystems will face even greater threats in the coming years. Policymakers are now under mounting pressure to allocate more resources toward early-warning systems, forest management, and community preparedness to mitigate the escalating crisis.
