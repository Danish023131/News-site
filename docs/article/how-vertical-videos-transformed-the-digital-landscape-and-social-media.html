How Vertical Videos Transformed the Digital Landscape and Social Media

The Verge · Jul 26, 2026

In the early days of the internet, digital platforms maintained strict and distinct identities. Users visited specific websites for particular types of content: YouTube served as a hub for traditional horizontal videos and independent creators, Instagram functioned primarily as a photo-sharing network, Facebook connected friends, and Twitter delivered breaking news. Today, however, that fragmented ecosystem has largely dissolved, replaced by a unifying format that has taken over the digital world—the vertical video.

The shift toward vertical viewing was catalyzed by smartphones, which users naturally hold upright. As mobile consumption surpassed desktop browsing, tech companies quickly realized that forcing users to rotate their devices created friction. Platforms like TikTok popularized the endless scrolling of short, vertical clips, prompting giants such as Instagram with Reels, YouTube with Shorts, and even Netflix to adapt their interfaces and content strategies.

This format revolution did more than just change screen orientations; it fundamentally altered how creators produce content and how audiences consume media. Algorithms are now finely tuned to deliver rapid-fire, vertical entertainment tailored to individual preferences, blurring the lines between what different apps offer. While critics argue this has led to a homogenization of social media apps, proponents highlight the unprecedented engagement and accessibility it brings to independent content creators worldwide. As the technology continues to evolve, the vertical video format remains the undisputed king of modern digital interaction.
