US Senate Bill Proposes Steep Tariffs on Russian Energy, Impacting Trade Partners Like India and China

Al Jazeera · Jul 31, 2026

Washington is currently reviewing a legislative proposal that could drastically alter global trade dynamics regarding Russian energy supplies. Introduced as a measure to tighten economic pressure on Moscow, the bill, named in honor of the late Senator Lindsey Graham, aims to impose severe financial penalties on countries that continue to purchase crude oil, natural gas, and other energy products from Russia.

At the center of this legislative debate is the potential imposition of tariffs reaching up to 100 percent on energy imports originating from Russia. While Western nations have already implemented various embargoes and price caps in response to the ongoing conflict in Ukraine, this new policy targets third-party nations that have maintained or even increased their trade volumes with Moscow. Among the most vulnerable economies are emerging global giants like India and China, which have significantly ramped up their intake of discounted Russian crude over the past few years.

Economic analysts point out that if the bill successfully passes through the US Senate and is enacted into law, it could create a major dilemma for New Delhi and Beijing. These nations have consistently defended their sovereign right to pursue independent trade policies aligned with their national energy security needs. Facing a potential 100 percent tariff barrier in the lucrative American market could force both governments to reassess their supply chains and trade strategies.

Furthermore, international trade experts warn that such aggressive secondary sanctions might lead to broader market disruptions, higher global energy prices, and increased geopolitical friction between Washington and major Asian capitals. As the legislative process moves forward, policymakers around the world will be closely monitoring how this bill evolves and whether exemptions or softer diplomatic alternatives might be considered to mitigate potential economic fallout.
