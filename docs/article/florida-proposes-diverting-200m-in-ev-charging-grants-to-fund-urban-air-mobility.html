Florida Proposes Diverting $200M in EV Charging Grants to Fund Urban Air Mobility Terminals

TechCrunch · Jul 31, 2026

In a controversial policy shift, state authorities in Florida have proposed reallocating a substantial $200 million federal grant, originally earmarked for electric vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure, toward the development of an advanced air taxi network. According to recent plans, the substantial funds would instead be utilized to construct vertiports and landing pads designed to accommodate futuristic passenger drones and electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft.

The ambitious infrastructure initiative aims to connect high-traffic areas across the state, specifically targeting luxury residential complexes, major aviation hubs, and popular golf resorts. Proponents of the project argue that integrating flying taxis into Florida’s transit ecosystem will significantly alleviate chronic highway congestion and modernize urban mobility. By linking key commercial and recreational zones through the skies, state planners envision a faster, more efficient travel alternative for both residents and tourists.

However, this strategic pivot has drawn immediate scrutiny and criticism from environmental advocates and clean energy proponents. Critics point out that diverting funds away from standard EV charging stations could stall the broader adoption of electric cars, undermining statewide sustainability goals. Electric vehicle infrastructure remains a critical component of national efforts to reduce carbon emissions, and shifting federal subsidies to benefit a nascent luxury transit sector has raised questions regarding federal compliance and equitable resource distribution.

As federal regulators review the proposal, the debate highlights a growing tension between investing in immediate, ground-level green transitions and funding speculative, next-generation aerial technologies. Whether Florida will successfully secure approval to repurpose the multi-million dollar federal allocation remains to be seen, but the decision could set a significant precedent for how states manage federal green energy grants moving forward.
