Google Fixes Record Chrome Vulnerabilities in June Using AI Technology

TechCrunch · Jul 31, 2026

Artificial intelligence is rapidly transforming cybersecurity and software development. Tech giant Google recently announced a significant milestone in its browser security efforts, revealing that it successfully addressed and patched a greater number of Chrome browser vulnerabilities in the month of June alone than the total number of bugs fixed over the preceding two years combined. This dramatic acceleration in vulnerability management is directly attributed to the integration of advanced Large Language Models (LLMs) and AI-driven diagnostic systems.

For the past couple of years, cybersecurity analysts and industry experts have predicted that machine learning tools would fundamentally alter how organizations identify and resolve software flaws. Companies like Microsoft and Google are now validating these predictions by deploying AI to scan codebases at unprecedented speeds. Traditional vulnerability detection often relied heavily on manual code reviews and conventional automated testing, which, while effective, were limited by human bandwidth and time constraints.

By incorporating LLMs into their security workflows, engineering teams can analyze massive volumes of code, anticipate potential security gaps, and generate patches much faster than ever before. This shift not only helps developers stay ahead of malicious actors but also significantly strengthens the overall security posture of widely used software like the Google Chrome browser. As AI technologies continue to mature across the tech industry, experts anticipate that automated vulnerability detection and repair will become the standard practice for major software developers worldwide, ultimately leading to a much safer digital ecosystem for everyday internet users.
