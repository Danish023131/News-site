West Africa Emerges as New Hub for Mexican Cartels Expanding Meth Production

BBC World · Jul 29, 2026

International drug enforcement agencies have raised serious concerns over a shifting trend in the global narcotics trade, as powerful Mexican cartels increasingly outsource their methamphetamine production to West Africa. According to recent reports, nations within the region, most notably Nigeria, are becoming primary destinations for illicit drug manufacturing and subsequent international distribution.

For years, South American and Mexican syndicates dominated the production of synthetic drugs like methamphetamine within their home territories before smuggling them across borders. However, heightened domestic scrutiny, stricter chemical import controls, and heavy law enforcement pressure in Latin America have forced these criminal organizations to adapt. They are now establishing clandestine laboratories thousands of miles away, tapping into regions with vulnerable governance and porous borders.

West Africa offers several strategic advantages for these cartels. The region provides easier access to precursor chemicals required for manufacturing synthetic drugs, often diverted from legitimate industrial supplies. Additionally, proximity to lucrative European and Asian markets makes West Africa an ideal transit corridor for smugglers seeking to bypass traditional interdiction routes.

Security experts and global intelligence agencies warn that this decentralization of drug manufacturing poses a severe threat to regional stability in West Africa. The influx of transnational organized crime risks fueling local corruption, increasing violent crime, and straining under-resourced law enforcement infrastructure in developing nations.

Efforts are currently underway by international bodies and local authorities to enhance intelligence sharing and border security to counter this evolving threat. However, as long as global demand for synthetic drugs remains high, criminal networks will likely continue exploiting new geographic frontiers to sustain their illicit operations.
